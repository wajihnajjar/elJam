Step Into Ancient Rome at El Jem

Walk the corridors of gladiators and emperors in one of the most impressive and best-preserved Roman ruins in the world.

The Crown Jewel of Roman Africa

The Amphitheatre of El Jem is an exceptionally well-preserved Roman colosseum located in the small town of El Jem, Tunisia. Built around 238 AD, it is the largest colosseum in North Africa and could hold up to 35,000 spectators. Walking through its massive stone arches, you are instantly transported back to a time of epic gladiator battles and chariot races.

Historical Highlights

The Underground Tunnels

Descend into the dark, intact underground corridors where gladiators and wild animals were kept before entering the arena.

El Jem Museum

Visit the nearby archaeological museum to view some of the most exquisite and vibrant Roman mosaics ever discovered in Africa.

Experience the magical acoustics of the arena during the International Festival of Symphonic Music held here every summer.

A Hollywood Star

The amphitheatre was famously used as a filming location for several movies, including the blockbuster "Gladiator" and "Life of Brian".

UNESCO Heritage

It was declared a World Heritage Site in 1979 because it is one of the most accomplished examples of Roman architecture in the world.

Built from Scratch

Unlike other amphitheatres built against hillsides, El Jem is entirely freestanding, built on flat ground using massive stone blocks.

Journey Through Time

Trace the epic history of El Jem from its Roman foundations to modern times.

238 AD

Construction of the Amphitheatre begins under the proconsul Gordian I, intended to showcase the wealth of the olive oil-rich city of Thysdrus.

3rd Century

The arena hosts spectacular gladiator combats, wild beast hunts, and chariot races, entertaining up to 35,000 spectators.

17th Century

After serving as a fortress for centuries, parts of the stone structure are dismantled by locals to build the nearby city of Kairouan and local mosques.

1979

The Amphitheatre of El Jem is officially declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognizing its status as a masterpiece of Roman architecture.
